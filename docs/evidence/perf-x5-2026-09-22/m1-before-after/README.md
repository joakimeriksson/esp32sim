# M1 Pro: before x4 versus after x5

The reviewed post-x5 artifact completed the same work in less wall time in all 16 primary before/after pairs. The observed reductions were **15.49% Pocket Tank / 14.39% TinyDraw in Chrome** and **24.08% / 11.62% in Safari**. This was an in-use machine: compilation overlapped part of Chrome, Chrome ran on battery and the selected Safari run began on AC power. These observations do not isolate the causal effect of the code or the browser engine. [All results](results.json), [conditions](conditions.json)

“Before” is pre-x4 source `7828e683`, artifact `37c13458`; “after” is reviewed post-x5 source `0ccb8337`, artifact `eb754176`. Both use inline threshold 2000, release DEBUG=0, STRIP=debuginfo and no wasm-opt, with matching firmware assets and runtime JavaScript. This measures the aggregate change across x4 and x5 under one build policy, not x5 alone or the size-default change. [Source and input identities](provenance.json)

Each row contains four alternating pairs. Less wall time is `100 × (1 − after median / before median)`; more throughput is `100 × (before median / after median − 1)` for the same completed work. The headline uses the ratio of medians, not the median of pair percentages. [Chrome Pocket](chrome/before-after-pocket.json), [Chrome TinyDraw](chrome/before-after-tinydraw.json), [Safari Pocket](safari/before-after-pocket.json), [Safari TinyDraw](safari/before-after-tinydraw.json)

For **Pocket Tank**, 30 guest seconds took 33.74837 host seconds in Chrome and 42.06995 in Safari after x5: **0.889× and 0.713× realtime**. Reaching 1× at those observed rates would require **12.49% and 40.23% more throughput**, respectively. These are arithmetic gaps, not predicted optimization gains. The battery harness counts guest frames without painting them to the page, so these are unrendered workload rates, not interactive display FPS or rendering latency. ## Controls, correctness and conditions

The two-pair A/A controls use the identical before artifact on both sides. Their apparent reductions were **+2.82% Pocket / −1.13% TinyDraw in Chrome** and **+1.85% / +0.86% in Safari**. These small controls do not establish a noise floor or justify subtracting a correction from the primary results. One warm-up pair per workload/browser is retained separately and excluded from the primary estimates. [Chrome controls](chrome/control-pocket.json), [Chrome TinyDraw control](chrome/control-tinydraw.json), [Safari controls](safari/control-pocket.json), [Safari TinyDraw control](safari/control-tinydraw.json)

The two complete browser campaigns contain **12 jobs and 56 arms**: 32 primary arms, 16 control arms and 8 warm-up arms. Every arm passes pinned instruction/frame counts, firmware checks, matching within-job console hashes and zero JIT failures. Pocket retires 10,073,833,775 instructions with 737 frames; TinyDraw retires 9,819,885,134 with 428 frames. These browser captures do not include frame-content hashes and therefore do not establish pixel equality. The public verifier recomputes the medians and pair reductions and checks artifact identities. Curation also checked the recorded input hashes; that check is retained in [curation verification](verification.json) and [input provenance](provenance.json). [Verification](verification.json), [re-runnable verifier](verify.mjs), [validation results](local-validation.json)

Chrome used a fresh headless process and dedicated profile per arm with background throttling disabled. Safari used an ordinary visible page with a fresh page and worker per arm; the browser process and caches could persist. Its harness rejects hidden-page arms. No WebDriver timing arm ran: Remote Automation was unavailable and its setting was left unchanged. These methods, their different power conditions and uncontrolled concurrent work prevent a clean browser-engine comparison. [Retained methods](harness-provenance.json), [attempt history](conditions.json)

Compilation was reported and observed during part of Chrome, and the requested campaign continued. Later compiler snapshots, including Safari snapshots, recorded zero matching compiler processes; that does not establish an otherwise idle machine. Aggregate load and one aggregate CPU sample are retained without process identities. Chrome's power snapshots were on battery. The first Safari attempt began on battery at 16%, then was interrupted after two completed warm-up arms. Those samples remain in the attempt history and are excluded from the complete campaign. Safari restarted from the beginning on AC power at 28%; the final snapshot remained on AC at 57%. No samples were adjusted to account for contention.
