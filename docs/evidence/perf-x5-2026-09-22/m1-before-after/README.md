# M1 Pro: before x4 versus after x5

**Measurement scope:** the M1 source `0ccb8337` and the M3 artifacts compared here predate the x4 correctness review fixes beginning at `6a13ddf0` and recorded through `5cc58493`. Those fixes change generated WASM. These historical results do not measure the corrected source, and no new corrected-source speed claim is made. [x4 corrections](../../perf-x4-2026-09-22/review-fixes.md), [artifact limitation](../../perf-x4-2026-09-22/review-validation.json).

The historical post-x5 artifact completed the same work in less wall time in the primary before/after pairs. The observed reductions were **15.49% Pocket Tank / 14.39% TinyDraw in Chrome**, **24.08% / 11.62% in Safari** and **17.49% / 9.29% in Firefox**. This was an in-use machine: compilation overlapped part of Chrome, which ran on battery. The selected Safari restart began on AC power; later Firefox snapshots also showed AC. These observations do not isolate the causal effect of the code or the browser engine. [All results](results.json), [conditions](conditions.json)

“Before” is pre-x4 source `7828e683`, artifact `37c13458`; “after” is historical post-x5 source `0ccb8337`, artifact `eb754176`. Both use inline threshold 2000, release DEBUG=0, STRIP=debuginfo and no wasm-opt, with matching firmware assets and runtime JavaScript. This measures the aggregate change across x4 and x5 under one build policy, not x5 alone or the size-default change. [Source and input identities](provenance.json)

For **Pocket Tank**, 30 guest seconds took 33.74837 host seconds in Chrome, 42.06995 in Safari and 54.16708 in Firefox after x5: **0.889×, 0.713× and 0.554× realtime**. Reaching 1× at those observed rates would require **12.49%, 40.23% and 80.56% more throughput**, respectively. These are arithmetic gaps, not predicted optimization gains. The battery harness counts guest frames without painting them to the page, so these are unrendered workload rates, not interactive display FPS or rendering latency. ## Controls, correctness and conditions

The two-pair A/A controls use the identical before artifact on both sides. Their apparent reductions were **+2.82% Pocket / −1.13% TinyDraw in Chrome**, **+1.85% / +0.86% in Safari** and **-0.53% / -3.50% in Firefox**. These small controls do not establish a noise floor or justify subtracting a correction from the primary results. One warm-up pair per workload/browser is retained separately and excluded from the primary estimates. [Chrome controls](chrome/control-pocket.json), [Chrome TinyDraw control](chrome/control-tinydraw.json), [Safari controls](safari/control-pocket.json), [Safari TinyDraw control](safari/control-tinydraw.json), [Firefox Pocket control](firefox/control-pocket.json), [Firefox TinyDraw control](firefox/control-tinydraw.json)

The three complete browser campaigns contain **18 jobs and 84 arms**: 48 primary arms, 24 control arms and 12 warm-up arms. Previously published Chrome/Safari job receipts remain byte-identical; their earlier aggregate metadata is preserved under [history](history/chrome-safari/results.json). Every arm passes pinned instruction/frame counts, firmware checks, matching within-job console hashes and zero JIT failures. Pocket retires 10,073,833,775 instructions with 737 frames; TinyDraw retires 9,819,885,134 with 428 frames. These browser captures do not include frame-content hashes and therefore do not establish pixel equality. The public verifier recomputes the medians and pair reductions and checks artifact identities. Curation also checked the recorded input hashes; that check is retained in [curation verification](verification.json) and [input provenance](provenance.json). [Verification](verification.json), [re-runnable verifier](verify.mjs), [validation results](local-validation.json)

Chrome used a fresh headless process and dedicated profile per arm with background throttling disabled. Safari and Firefox used ordinary visible pages with a fresh page and worker per arm; browser processes and caches could persist. Both harnesses reject hidden-page arms. Firefox uses SpiderMonkey; no separate SpiderMonkey version was measured. No Safari WebDriver timing arm ran: Remote Automation was unavailable and its setting was left unchanged. These methods, their different power conditions and uncontrolled concurrent work prevent a clean browser-engine comparison. [Chrome/Safari methods](harness-provenance.json), [Firefox method](firefox-harness-provenance.json), [attempt history](conditions.json)

Compilation was reported and observed during part of Chrome, and the requested campaign continued. Later compiler snapshots, including Safari snapshots, recorded zero matching compiler processes; that does not establish an otherwise idle machine. Aggregate load and one aggregate CPU sample are retained without process identities. Chrome's power snapshots were on battery. The first Safari attempt began on battery at 16%, then was interrupted after two completed warm-up arms. Those samples remain in the attempt history and are excluded from the complete campaign. Safari restarted from the beginning on AC power at 28%; the final snapshot remained on AC at 57%. Firefox observations showed AC power at 76% before its first attempt, 85% after five completed restart arms and 98% after completion. No dedicated restart-start power snapshot was recorded. Its compiler spot counts were zero; those observations do not establish otherwise idle conditions. No samples were adjusted to account for contention. [Condition samples and excluded attempts](conditions.json)

The first Firefox attempt completed one baseline warm-up workload, then its copied comparator rejected the result because it still required Safari metadata. This was an adapter validation error, not a firmware or browser execution failure. That arm remains separately retained and excluded; the corrected campaign restarted from the beginning. [Attempt record](conditions.json)
